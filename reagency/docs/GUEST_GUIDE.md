# World of Shadow Work — a guide for guests

*An immersive AlloSphere piece. About 14 minutes, looping. Sound and image are generated live —
no two moments are exactly the same.*

---

## What you're standing inside
You're surrounded by a slowly turning **galaxy of images** — thousands of pictures an AI has
sorted into a glowing 3-D cloud, with bright threads connecting the ones it thinks are alike.
It looks like a mind dreaming. It's beautiful on purpose.

That beauty is the hook. The piece is about the **hidden labor** behind it — the people whose
unseen work makes "the magic" possible, and whom we rarely look for. **"Shadow work"** is that
invisible, uncredited labor (and, underneath, the shadow each of us would rather not face).

## The four voices of the piece
Without needing to catch every word, this is the emotional shape:
- **ME** — the machine, seeming to speak. *(It can't really confess — a human wrote its lines.)*
- **YOU** — you, the visitor. You loved the galaxy. You didn't go looking for the workers.
- **THEM** — the real, named workers who labeled the data — held deliberately *apart*, never
  dissolved into a comfortable "we."
- **US** — those of us who benefit from THEM.

## What you'll see
- **The galaxy** — the cloud of images all around you; the AI's map of what it has seen.
- **The gaze** — a point of attention drifts along the threads. When it settles on an image,
  that **photo surfaces**, then **dissolves into a stream of grains** that pours back into the
  cloud — the human picture becoming machine fuel.
- **Dreams** — pictures that **form out of noise**, like watching the AI imagine. These are its
  confabulations: things it "saw" that were never real.
- **The vessel** — a shifting cloud of points at the center that keeps trying to become a shape.
- **The names** — as the piece goes on, the **workers' names** appear and stay — they don't fade.

## What you'll hear
A living score that grows from the same data you're seeing — a deep drone, shimmering tones, and
**ghostly voices**: fragments of real workers' testimony. On the dome the sound moves **around
you** in space, with the bass felt from below. The voices are *THEM*, made audible.

## The arc — five acts
| | Act | What shifts |
|---|---|---|
| **I** | **Seduction** | The galaxy is gorgeous and inviting; warm, enveloping sound. You fall for it. |
| **II** | **Reading** | The machine starts *labeling* — words stamp onto the images as it classifies what it sees. |
| **III** | **Extraction** | The grind. Images are consumed faster, dissolving into grains; the sound hardens — the cost surfaces, and the workers' names begin to appear. |
| **IV** | **The Turn** | Everything drops to a bare pulse and near-silence. This is the mirror — *you used it; did you look for them?* |
| **V** | **Residue** | It brightens again — but only **partway**. It never returns to the Act-I bliss. The workers' names remain, haunting the comfort. Then it loops. |

## The turn is yours
**Act IV listens to you.** If you grow still and simply *look* — in the dome, it senses your
gaze and stillness — the piece deepens its plunge and turns the question on you. The most
important moment isn't something it shows you; it's whether **you stop to look for the people who
aren't in the picture.**

---

*World of Shadow Work asks you to love the machine's beautiful surface — and then to feel the
weight of everyone underneath it who never got to.*

---

## Wall card (one paragraph)

**World of Shadow Work** — Step inside a galaxy the machine dreamed: thousands of images an AI has
sorted into a glowing cloud, wrapped in a score that grows from the same data. It's gorgeous on
purpose. Over about fourteen minutes it seduces you, then turns — because the beauty rests on
**hidden human labor**, the uncredited workers who labeled the data that trains the magic. Their
names surface and stay. The piece asks one quiet question: you loved it — *did you look for them?*

## Docent script (~30 seconds, spoken)

> "Welcome. You're stepping inside a galaxy of images an AI sorted — it surrounds you, it's
> beautiful, and the sound is made live, so it's never the same twice. It runs about fourteen
> minutes and loops, so come in anytime. One thing to know: all that beauty rests on **hidden
> human labor** — the real workers who labeled the data behind it. Watch for their names; they
> appear and don't fade. And near the end it goes quiet and turns toward you — if you stop and
> really look, that's the moment. Enjoy."
